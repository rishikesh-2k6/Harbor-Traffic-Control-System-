WHAT IS THIS PROJECT IN ONE SENTENCE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A real-time 3D simulation of a smart harbor that uses UNDERWATER ACOUSTIC MODEMS
(not WiFi, not Bluetooth) to form a sensor network that detects, classifies, and
manages maritime vessel traffic - powered by machine learning and robotics.


================================================================================
SECTION 1: WHY NOT WIFI OR BLUETOOTH? (THE CORE MOTIVATION)
================================================================================

This is the most important concept to explain to your professor.

WiFi and Bluetooth are RADIO WAVES. Radio waves travel through air extremely
well. But water is a completely different story:

  WHAT HAPPENS TO RADIO WAVES UNDERWATER:
  ----------------------------------------
  - Seawater is a conductor (salt + water = electrical conductor)
  - Radio frequency (RF) signals are attenuated (absorbed) EXPONENTIALLY
    by seawater
  - At 2.4 GHz (WiFi frequency): signal dies within a FEW CENTIMETRES
  - At 2.4 GHz (Bluetooth): same problem - dies within centimetres
  - Even at lower RF frequencies (VLF), you need MASSIVE antenna arrays
    like military submarines use

  OUR SOLUTION - UNDERWATER ACOUSTIC MODEMS:
  -------------------------------------------
  - Sound waves travel through water VERY WELL (like how dolphins communicate!)
  - In seawater, sound travels at ~1500 m/s (vs. 343 m/s in air)
  - Acoustic modems operate at ~12,000 Hz (12 kHz) carrier frequency
  - Communication range: up to 1,800 metres (R_comm = 1.8 km)
  - Detection range: up to 2,000 metres (R_detect = 2.0 km)
  - Bitrate: ~1000 bps (slow, but it WORKS underwater!)
  - Transmit power: 10 Watts per modem

  WHERE WE USE THIS IN CODE (simulator.py):
  ------------------------------------------
    AUV_COMM_FREQ_HZ = 12000         # 12 kHz acoustic carrier
    AUV_COMM_SOURCE_LEVEL = 178.0    # dB re 1 uPa @ 1m (transmit power)
    LINK_SNR_THRESHOLD_DB = 10.0     # minimum signal-to-noise ratio needed
    AUV_COMM_RANGE_M = 1800          # max communication radius in metres

  REAL PHYSICS EQUATIONS USED:
  - Mackenzie (1981): sound speed in water (depends on temp, salinity, depth)
  - Thorp (1967): acoustic absorption loss in dB per km
  - Ross (1976): ship radiated noise (how loud a ship is acoustically)
  - Wenz (1962): ambient ocean noise model (wind + shipping + biological)
  - Nystuen rain noise model: +15 dB from monsoon rain hitting the surface

SECTION 2: THE BIG PICTURE - WHAT THE SIMULATION DOES
================================================================================

Imagine a harbor (10 km x 10 km area) divided into 3 zones:

  OUTER YARD (x > 5000 m)     - Speed limit: 10 knots, open sea approach
  INNER ZONE (700m < x < 5000m) - Speed limit: 6 knots, AUV patrol zone
  HARBOR     (x < 700 m)      - Docking area, berths by vessel type

The simulation runs at 60 frames per second with:
  - 22 vessels (Cargo Ships, Tankers, Cruisers, Ferries, Speedboats, Fishing)
  - 35 underwater hydrophone sensor nodes on the seabed
  - 8 Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) patrolling
  - 3 surface buoy gateways (Buoy-Alpha, Buoy-Beta, Buoy-Gamma)


================================================================================
SECTION 3: THE ACOUSTIC SENSOR NETWORK (network.py)
================================================================================

HOW A LINK IS ESTABLISHED (critical concept):
  Two nodes can talk ONLY IF:
    1. Distance <= 1800 metres (within acoustic range)
    2. SNR >= 10 dB (signal strong enough above background noise)

  The SNR formula:
    SNR = Source_Level - Transmission_Loss - Ambient_Noise

  Transmission Loss = Spherical Spreading + Thorp Absorption
    = 20 * log10(range_m) + absorption_coefficient * range_km

DEPTH-BASED ROUTING (DBR) - Our Routing Protocol:
  - Data "floats upward" towards the surface
  - Each node forwards data to a neighbor closer to the surface (higher z)
  - Uses Dijkstra shortest path (NetworkX library)
  - Multi-hop: sensor -> sensor -> AUV -> buoy (2 to 8 hops)
  - This protocol is designed specifically for underwater networks

MULTI-GATEWAY DIVERSITY:
  3 buoys (Alpha, Beta, Gamma) provide redundancy.
  If one path is degraded, traffic reroutes through another gateway.

DUTY CYCLING (Sleep/Wake Cycles):
  - Sensors sleep to save battery, waking periodically
  - Wake-up uses an acoustic PREAMBLE (a tone sent before each packet)
  - Normal season: 94% availability
  - Monsoon season: rain noise masks preamble -> only 65% availability
  - This is a real challenge in underwater sensor networks!

KEY NETWORK PARAMETERS:
  V_SOUND = 1500 m/s            # Speed of sound in seawater
  BITRATE_BPS = 1000            # ~1 kbps data rate (slow but underwater!)
  SIZE_DEFAULT = 256 bytes      # Standard packet
  SIZE_COMPRESSED = 16 bytes    # Compressed packet (header only)
  Trans_delay = 256*8/1000 = 2.048 seconds per hop! (vs. WiFi microseconds)

SECTION 4: PACKET TRANSMISSION - WHAT YOU SEE ON SCREEN
================================================================================

When a sensor detects a vessel, a DATA PACKET is created and travels
hop-by-hop through the acoustic network. You see this in real time:

VISUAL INDICATORS:
  Moving dot (*)    = packet in transit
  Green dashed line = strong link (SNR >= 20 dB)
  Cyan dashed line  = marginal link (SNR 12-20 dB)
  Red dashed line   = weak link (SNR < 12 dB), risk of drop
  Red X symbol      = PACKET DROPPED (acoustic failure)

PACKET LIFECYCLE:
  IN TRANSIT -> hops are progressing visually
  DELIVERED  -> reached the buoy gateway successfully
  DROPPED    -> lost due to insufficient SNR on a hop

WHAT IS MEASURED PER PACKET:
  - SNR on each hop (dB) - is the signal strong enough?
  - Transmission delay per hop (seconds) - how long does each hop take?
  - Energy consumed per hop (Joules) - battery cost per transmission
  - Which hop failed (if dropped)

SECTION 5: MONSOON vs. NORMAL - THE KEY DEMO
================================================================================

NORMAL SEASON:
  Sea state 2-3, wind 8-16 knots, no rain noise
  -> SNR is high, links are stable, 94% sensors active
  -> Network: STABLE, packet delivery ~90%+

MONSOON SEASON:
  Sea state 6-8, wind 36-52 knots, +15 dB rain noise, +9 dB bubble loss
  -> Total SNR degradation: up to 24 dB
  -> A link with 20 dB SNR now has -4 dB = COMPLETELY DEAD
  -> Only 65% sensors available (preamble masked by rain noise)
  -> Network: DEGRADED -> CRITICAL -> COLLAPSED

SQUALL EVENTS (random, non-deterministic):
  - 2.5% chance per frame of a violent gale squall
  - Current spikes +1.8 to +3.4 m/s randomly
  - Sensors snap off their moorings and drift away
  - Too many drifted sensors = NETWORK COLLAPSED
  - Solution: click "Reanchor Sensors" to restore the network

THE KEY DEMONSTRATION POINT FOR YOUR PROFESSOR:
  "Even acoustic modems - the best technology available for underwater
   communication - struggle severely in monsoon conditions. If we had
   tried to use WiFi or Bluetooth, it would have failed completely even
   in calm conditions. This simulation proves exactly WHY underwater
   networking requires specialized protocols, acoustic modems, and
   intelligent routing like DBR."

SECTION 6: ML COMPONENT - VESSEL CLASSIFICATION (mlmodel.py)
================================================================================

WHY ML?
  A hydrophone hears a ship but doesn't know WHAT TYPE it is.
  Different ships have different acoustic signatures.
  We train a neural network to classify them from sensor readings.

THE MODEL:
  - Architecture: VesselMLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron in PyTorch)
  - Runs on GPU (CUDA) if available, otherwise CPU
  - Input: 18 features (speed, heading, acoustic source level,
    Doppler shift, bearing, range, depth, temperature, salinity...)
  - Output 1: Vessel Type (6 classes)
  - Output 2: Vessel Weight in kg (regression)

ONLINE RETRAINING:
  - Every detection is saved to collected_data.csv (live data growing)
  - After 100+ new rows, the model automatically retrains
  - Validation gate: new model accepted only if validation loss improves
  - So the system learns and improves in real time!

DOPPLER SHIFT:
  Moving ships shift the acoustic frequency (like ambulance siren effect):
    f_received = f_source * (v_sound / (v_sound - v_radial))
  This is captured as a feature to help classify vessel motion patterns.

================================================================================

8 AUVs act as MOBILE NETWORK RELAY NODES:
  - Fill communication gaps between fixed sensors
  - Intercept vessels violating speed/zone rules
  - Recharge at surface buoys when battery low

AUV PHYSICS:
  Max speed: 10 m/s | Mass: 500 kg | Max thrust: 900 N
  Drag: 0.85 | Battery drain: 0.015%/frame | Charge: 0.2%/frame

BEHAVIORS:
  PATROLLING  = moving waypoint-to-waypoint in assigned zone
  DISPATCHED  = intercepting a violating vessel
  CHARGING    = returning to surface buoy for recharge

SECTION 11: Q&A CHEAT SHEET
================================================================================

Q: Why not WiFi underwater?
A: WiFi = 2.4 GHz radio. Seawater conducts electricity and absorbs radio
   waves within centimetres. Sound travels 1500 m/s through water - much better.

Q: What frequency do the modems use?
A: 12,000 Hz (12 kHz) - in the audio range, similar to a very high-pitched sound.

Q: What is the communication range?
A: 1,800 metres (1.8 km) per acoustic modem hop.

Q: What data rate?
A: 1,000 bits per second (1 kbps). Very slow vs WiFi (1 Gbps) but works!

Q: What routing protocol?
A: Depth-Based Routing (DBR). Data hops upward toward the surface gateway.

Q: What is SNR and why does it matter?
A: Signal-to-Noise Ratio in dB. If SNR < 10 dB, the signal is too weak
   to decode. Background noise (waves, rain, ships) drowns out the signal.

Q: How does monsoon affect the network?
A: +15 dB rain noise + 9 dB bubble scattering = up to 24 dB more noise.
   Links that worked fine go dead. Sensor preamble wake-up fails.

Q: What is duty cycling?
A: Sensors sleep to save battery, waking periodically for an acoustic
   preamble. In monsoon, rain noise masks preambles = sensors miss wake-up.

Q: What does the ML model do?
A: Classifies vessel type from acoustic signatures using a PyTorch neural
   network, retrained live as more detections accumulate.

Q: What do AUVs do?
A: Mobile relay nodes that fill network coverage gaps, intercept violating
   vessels, and recharge at surface buoys when battery is low.

Q: What is the network health score?
A: 0-100% based on gateway reachability, packet loss rate, failed node
   count, and delays. States: STABLE / DEGRADED / CRITICAL / COLLAPSED.
